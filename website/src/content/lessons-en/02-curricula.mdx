## The curricula of the Master’s degree

Since 2017 the Master’s degree in Computer Science has been organized into four curricula:

- **Artificial Intelligence** (AI);
- **Data and Knowledge**, since 2021 **Big Data Technologies** (BD);
- **ICT Solutions Architect** (ICT);
- **Software: Programming, Principles, and Technologies** (SW).

The organization into curricula makes it possible to specialize in one area (with a diploma supplement certifying the curriculum), offers basic methodological courses at the beginning of the program and aims to show how two more years of study are useful for one’s professional future. Regulations, instructions for the exams and the procedures for changing curriculum or study plan are on the department’s website.

## The role of Machine Learning

ML is connected to **all four** curricula, but it is a **core course of the AI curriculum**.

The other core courses of the AI curriculum are *Human Language Technologies*, *Intelligent Systems for Pattern Recognition* and *Smart Applications*, alongside groups of elective exams (Algorithm Engineering, Data Mining, Mobile and Cyber-Physical Systems, Information Retrieval, Computational Neuroscience, Robotics, Semantic Web, etc.).

ML is the pivot of the **intelligent systems area**: many courses of the Master’s degree — Human Language Technologies, Intelligent Systems for Pattern Recognition, Smart Applications, Computational Neuroscience, Robotics, Data Mining, Information Retrieval, Bioinformatics, Big Data Analytics… — directly use its methods.

## Synergy with Computational Mathematics

The professor recommends taking **ML** and **CM** (*Computational Mathematics for Learning and Data Analysis*) in parallel: the former provides the methods, the latter the mathematical foundations, and the two courses stimulate each other. CM is not a prerequisite, though: many students from other degree programs or curricula have taken ML without CM.

## Notes for particular categories of students

<Callout type="warning" title="For those coming from the Bachelor’s degree in Pisa (IIA course)">

The first six lectures will seem “easy”, but they **do not coincide** with the content of IIA: they contain new parts and different mathematical concepts. The change of perspective is sharp: in IIA simple models were the *tool* for introducing ML concepts, here state-of-the-art models and principles are *the object* of study. From lecture 5–6 onwards everything is new.

</Callout>

For students of the old **AA1** exam (320AA, 6 ECTS), which no longer exists: those who have to move from AA1 to ML (9 ECTS) take a supplementary exam on a list of topics indicated by the professor; those who have to go the other way must contact the lecturer.
