<Lead>This second part formalizes the correct use of cross-validation for model selection and/or risk estimation, with a precise notation (adapted from the slides of S. Bengio). These are simple concepts, but a rigorous notation avoids ambiguity. It is not a recipe book: its purpose is to **rationalize** and understand a rigorous approach in a systematic way, and then to be reasonable. The time needed to run the experiments depends above all on the validation choices: one must avoid both an evaluation that is too coarse and an endless evaluation process. When in doubt, these schemes are in any case better than imagination.</Lead>

The discussion is about selecting among configurations of <T id="iperparametri">hyperparameters</T> (denoted by $\theta$), but the same schemes apply to choosing among **different models** ($\theta$ can also represent the type of model).

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep the counterexample in mind">

From [part 1](#/lezione/09/un-controesempio-istruttivo): (1) the error estimates on TR and/or VL are **not** good estimates of the risk; (2) using the whole dataset for feature or model selection **compromises** the estimate. In the schemes that follow one can see exactly where these mistakes would be made.

</Callout>

## Notation

**Data**: $Z_1, \dots, Z_l$ is a random sample from an unknown distribution $p(z)$, with all the $Z_i$ <T id="iid">**independent and identically distributed**</T> (i.i.d.). $D_r = \{z_1, \dots, z_r\}$ is a particular instance with $r$ elements.

**Task**: classification, $Z = (X, Y) \in \mathbb{R}^n \times \{-1, +1\}$; regression, $Z = (X, Y) \in \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}$.

**Loss** (“internal”): classification $L((x,y), h) = 0$ if $h(x) = y$, $1$ otherwise; regression $L((x,y), h) = (h(x) - y)^2$.

**Goal**: minimize the <T id="rischio">**expected risk**</T> (true error) over $H$:

<Formula f={F.risk} />

The problem: $p(z)$ is unknown and we do not have access to all the $L(z,h)$.

<T id="rischio-empirico">**Empirical risk**</T> on a finite set $D_r$:

<Formula f={F.remp} />

<Callout type="warning" title={<>General use of <Tex>{'R_{emp}'}</Tex></>}>

Here $R_{emp}$ is meant in a general sense, as a **surrogate of $R$** on a finite set of data, used for training, model selection or assessment depending on the partition. It should not be confused with the $R_{emp}$ of SLT, which is specifically the training error: in this notation it is $R_{emp}(h, D_{TR})$.

</Callout>

## Methodology: identifying the goal

1. To provide the **best model** obtainable from a dataset? → <T id="model-selection">**model selection**</T> is needed.
2. To provide the **expected performance** of a model obtained by empirical risk minimization? → <T id="model-assessment">**model assessment**</T> (risk estimation) is needed.
3. To provide **both**? → both are needed.

The original dataset $D_l$ will be used for these different purposes.

## Model selection

### Hold-out

<Callout type="abstract" title="Model selection with hold-out">

- Choose a hypothesis space with hyperparameter $\theta$.
- Split $D_l$ into $D_{TR} = \{z_1, \dots, z_{tr}\}$ and $D_{VL} = \{z_{tr+1}, \dots, z_{tr+vl}\}$, with $tr + vl = l$.
- For each value $\theta_m$ [**grid search**]:
  - $h^*_{\theta_m}(D_{TR}) = \arg\min_{h \in H_{\theta_m}} R_{emp}(h, D_{TR})$ [**training**];
  - estimate $R(h^*_{\theta_m})$ with $R_{emp}(h^*_{\theta_m}, D_{VL}) = \frac{1}{vl}\sum_{z_i \in D_{VL}} L(z_i, h^*_{\theta_m}(D_{TR}))$.
- Choose $\theta^*_m = \arg\min_{\theta_m} R(h^*_{\theta_m})$ [best result on the VL].
- Return $h^*(D_l) = \arg\min_{h \in H_{\theta^*_m}} R_{emp}(h, D_l)$ [**retraining** on all $l$ data points].

</Callout>

The estimate of $R$ on the VL serves **only** for model selection and **is not returned** as an estimate of the risk: returning it would be exactly mistake (1) of the counterexample.

### K-fold cross-validation

<Callout type="abstract" title="Model selection with K-fold CV">

- Choose a hypothesis space with hyperparameter $\theta$.
- Split $D_l$ into $K$ distinct and equal parts $D_1, \dots, D_K$; let $\bar D_k$ be the complement of $D_k$.
- For each value $\theta_m$ [**grid search**]:
  - for each part $D_k$ [**loop over the folds**]:
    - $h^*_{\theta_m}(\bar D_k) = \arg\min_{h \in H_{\theta_m}} R_{emp}(h, \bar D_k)$ [training];
    - estimate $R(h^*_{\theta_m}(\bar D_k))$ with $R_{emp}(h^*_{\theta_m}(\bar D_k), D_k) = \frac{1}{|D_k|}\sum_{z_i \in D_k} L(z_i, h^*_{\theta_m}(\bar D_k))$;
  - estimate $R(h^*_{\theta_m}(D_l))$ with the mean $\frac{1}{K}\sum_k R(h^*_{\theta_m}(\bar D_k))$.
- Choose $\theta^*_m = \arg\min_{\theta_m} R(h^*_{\theta_m}(D_l))$ [best over all the CVs].
- Return $h^*(D_l) = \arg\min_{h \in H_{\theta^*_m}} R_{emp}(h, D_l)$ [retraining].

</Callout>

The estimate for each configuration is therefore the mean of the errors on the folds:

<Formula f={F.cvMean} />

<Callout type="warning" title="Mind the order">

Each iteration **starts from scratch**, with training and estimation local to the split. If, for example, a model trained in split 1 were used to evaluate split 2, the validation of split 2 would use a part ($D_2$) that had been used for training in split 1.

</Callout>

## Risk estimation (model assessment)

### Hold-out

<Callout type="abstract" title="Model assessment with hold-out">

- Split $D_l$ into $D_{TR}$ and $D_{TS}$, with $tr + ts = l$. **The test set must be set aside NOW** (not later): otherwise one makes mistake (2) of the counterexample.
- $h^*(D_{TR}) = \arg\min_{h \in H} R_{emp}(h, D_{TR})$ [training; it may include an internal model selection on $D_{TR}$].
- Estimate $R(h^*(D_{TR}))$ with $R_{emp}(h^*(D_{TR}), D_{TS}) = \frac{1}{ts}\sum_{z_i \in D_{TS}} L(z_i, h^*(D_{TR}))$ [**test error**].

</Callout>

### K-fold cross-validation

<Callout type="abstract" title="Model assessment with K-fold CV">

- Split $D_l$ into $K$ distinct and equal parts.
- For each part $D_k$ [loop over the folds]:
  - $h^*(\bar D_k) = \arg\min_{h \in H} R_{emp}(h, \bar D_k)$ [training; it may include a model selection];
  - estimate $R(h^*(\bar D_k))$ with $R_{emp}(h^*(\bar D_k), D_k)$ [test error of each iteration].
- Estimate $R(h^*(D_l))$ with the mean $\frac{1}{K}\sum_k R(h^*(\bar D_k))$.

</Callout>

With $K = l$ one obtains <T id="leave-one-out">**leave-one-out CV**</T>. The principles are not violated: the test results are never used for training or selection, in any iteration.

## Combining model selection and model assessment

When both the best model and its expected risk are wanted, the methods have to be combined. For example:

- **hold-out** with training–validation–test (three separate sets are needed);
- **K-fold CV + test**: cross-validation on the training set, then test on a separate set;
- **outer K-fold CV for the test**, with an inner hold-out for model selection;
- **double (nested) cross-validation**: for each outer fold, a nested cross-validation on the other $K-1$.

Other aspects: take the **different trials** (different initializations of a network) into account when computing the means on TR, VL, TS. More generally: compare the results with other methods, use **statistical tests** to check their significance, verify the model on several datasets.

### Hold-out training–validation–test

<Callout type="abstract" title="TR–VL–TS scheme">

- Split $D_l$ into $D_{TR}$, $D_{VL}$, $D_{TS}$.
- For each $\theta_m$: train $h^*_{\theta_m}(D_{TR})$ and compute $R_{emp}(h^*_{\theta_m}(D_{TR}), D_{VL})$.
- Choose $\theta^*_m$ with the best result on the VL.
- Retrain $h^*(D_{TR} \cup D_{VL})$ with $\theta^*_m$.
- Estimate $R$ with $\frac{1}{ts}\sum_{z_i \in D_{TS}} L(z_i, h^*(D_{TR} \cup D_{VL}))$.

</Callout>

The retraining can use part of the data as VL for <T id="early-stopping">early stopping</T>.

<Callout type="question" title={<>Can one retrain once more on the whole <Tex>{'D_l'}</Tex>?</>}>

The final retraining on all the data (TR+VL+TS) **does not use** the test results for any choice: the final model will in general perform as well as or better than the estimate (more data), which remains valid as a (slightly pessimistic) estimate of the risk of the procedure. What matters is that the test has not influenced any decision.

</Callout>

### K-fold CV for selection + hold-out for the test

<Callout type="abstract" title="CV + test scheme">

- Split $D_l$ into $D_{TR}$ (the <T id="design-set">*design set*</T>) and $D_{TS}$.
- For each $\theta_m$: estimate $R(h^*_{\theta_m}(D_{TR}))$ with a K-fold CV on $D_{TR}$ (mean validation error over the folds).
- Choose $\theta^*_m$ with the best estimate.
- Retrain $h^*(D_{TR})$.
- Estimate $R$ with the error on $D_{TS}$.

</Callout>

### K-fold CV for the test with an inner hold-out for selection

In each row of the outer K-fold, one fold acts as test and the rest is split (arbitrarily and with shuffling) into TR and VL for model selection. The description as a meta-algorithm is left as an exercise.

This process **does not provide a final model**: it only gives an estimate of the risk of the **class** of models considered, because potentially each row selects a different model.

</Callout>

### Double (nested) K-fold CV

<Callout type="abstract" title="Double K-fold CV">

- Split $D_l$ into $K$ distinct and equal parts.
- For each part $D_k$ [**outer loop**]:
  - choose $h^*(\bar D_k)$ with an **inner K-fold CV** on $\bar D_k$ (with $K'$ possibly different from $K$) [**inner loop**];
  - estimate $R(h^*(\bar D_k))$ with $R_{emp}(h^*(\bar D_k), D_k)$ [test error for each outer split].
- Estimate $R(h^*(D_l))$ with the mean over the test folds.

</Callout>

Click an outer split to see its inner CV: the mean validation errors for each degree $M$ and the choice.</Caption>

Here too **no single final model is obtained**, because each outer loop can choose different hyperparameters: what one obtains is an estimate of the risk of the class of models/algorithm, together with its **variance** (standard deviation over the folds). If a single final model is needed, a **separate model selection** (hold-out or K-fold CV) is run, followed by a possible final retraining.

This does not break the rules: the test error has already been estimated and the test results are never used for selection; the final model will have an expected error within the estimated interval. This is why it is not simply true that “the test always comes last”: here it takes place **before** the last model selection (even though it may seem counterintuitive).

<T id="double-cv">Double CV</T> makes better use of **all** the data for training and evaluation (both for selection and for evaluation), at the price of a high computational cost.

<Callout type="abstract" title="Summary">

First one identifies the goal (best model, performance estimate, or both), then one chooses the scheme. In all the correct schemes: the **test is set aside before** any choice, the estimates on the **VL are not returned** as estimates of the risk, each CV iteration **starts from scratch**. The most widely used combined schemes are TR–VL–TS, CV+test and double CV.

</Callout>

<Exam>
<Q q="Write the algorithm for model selection with K-fold CV.">

One chooses the hypothesis space with hyperparameter $\theta$ and splits $D_l$ into $K$ equal parts $D_1, \dots, D_K$. For each value $\theta_m$ of the grid and for each part $D_k$: $h^*_{\theta_m}(\bar D_k)$ is trained from scratch by minimizing $R_{emp}$ on $\bar D_k$ and its $R_{emp}$ is measured on $D_k$; the estimate for $\theta_m$ is the mean of the $K$ errors. One chooses $\theta^*_m$ with the lowest mean and returns the model retrained on the whole $D_l$ with $\theta^*_m$. The validation means are not returned as an estimate of the risk.

</Q>
<Q q="Write the algorithm for model assessment with K-fold CV.">

One splits $D_l$ into $K$ equal parts. For each part $D_k$ one obtains $h^*(\bar D_k)$ by training (possibly with an internal model selection) only on $\bar D_k$, and measures its test error $R_{emp}(h^*(\bar D_k), D_k)$. The estimate of the risk is the mean of the $K$ test errors (with the standard deviation). With $K = l$ it is leave-one-out. The test results are never used for training or for choosing.

</Q>
<Q q="Describe double (nested) CV. Does it return a model?">

One splits $D_l$ into $K$ outer folds; for each of them, an inner K-fold CV (with $K'$ possibly different) on $\bar D_k$ chooses the hyperparameters, the model is retrained on $\bar D_k$ and the error is measured on $D_k$; the mean (and the variance) of the outer errors estimates the risk. It does not return a single model, because each outer split can choose different hyperparameters: it estimates the risk of the class of models/algorithm. For a final model one runs a separate model selection (hold-out or K-fold) and retrains; this does not break the rules, because the test has not influenced any choice. It makes good use of all the data, but it is very expensive.

</Q>
<Q q="Connect mistakes (1) and (2) of the counterexample to the formal schemes.">

Mistake (1), using the estimates on TR or VL as an estimate of the risk: in the model selection schemes the estimate on the VL (or the mean of the CV) serves only to choose $\theta^*_m$ and is not returned. Mistake (2), performing the selection on the whole dataset: in the assessment schemes the test set must be set aside immediately, before any choice (including feature selection), and in the K-fold schemes the selection takes place inside each split, only on $\bar D_k$. The order matters too: each iteration starts from scratch, otherwise a validation fold would already have been seen in training.

</Q>
</Exam>
